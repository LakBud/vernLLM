---
title: Router and usage
description: How policy nests with the router and usage extensions, allowed versus preferred, and what a closed route list does when policy removes its targets.
---

The extensions connect through nesting only. Policy narrows `ctx.targets`, and what is inside sees
the smaller list. None imports another, and none needs a change for the others.

```text title="policy-nesting.txt"
ctx.targets    openai, anthropic, gemini
policy         pii removes openai          → next({ targets: ['anthropic', 'gemini'] })
router         sees anthropic, gemini      → next({ targets: ['gemini', 'anthropic'] })
core           tries gemini, falls back to anthropic, never to openai
```

## With the router

**Policy decides what is allowed. The router decides what is preferred.**

- Prefer an open list in routes, `targets: [['openai', 'anthropic'], '*']`. It keeps those first
  and lets anything else policy allowed follow
- Or express a preference as a strategy or `exclude`, and leave "only these" to policy rules
- Keep closed route lists for cases that really mean "only these, or fail"

A route that lists only targets policy removed leaves the router nothing to order. A closed list
fails the call with `no_eligible_targets`, and an open `'*'` keeps it alive on whatever is left.

The router warns once when a route names a target the call does not have, which is what a policy
removal looks like from inside the router. The warning is accurate, and not a bug to chase.

## With usage

Policy is outside usage, so a denied call reserves no budget and spends nothing. Usage prices the
hold at the most expensive target still allowed, so removing the costly targets also lowers what a
call reserves.

## With the logger

The logger sits outside policy and records denials at `warn` as `rejected`. It keeps the
`policy.*` events by default.

## With Hedge

A target policy removed never reaches `ctx.targets`, so it can never be hedged to. For content that
must not go to two providers, set a `duplicateSends` flag and skip the extra call with
`when: (ctx) => policyFlags(ctx).duplicateSends !== false`. See
[Recipes](/docs/extensions/hedge/features/recipes).
